## 1. Independent-Contractor Status

Couriers are independent contractors. Nothing in this Agreement creates employment, partnership, agency, or joint venture.

Couriers control:
- **when** they work;
- **whether** they accept any delivery;
- **how** they perform deliveries (route, method, transportation).

The Platform provides technology services only and does not control the manner or method of courier transportation.

### 1.1 Freedom to Work Elsewhere
Couriers are free to perform delivery or any other services for any other business or platform. Nothing in this Agreement restricts that freedom.

### 1.2 Marketplace Role — Capacity-First
The Platform is a digital marketplace that lets Senders request deliveries and lets independent couriers view and claim available delivery opportunities. The Platform does not assign deliveries, does not require couriers to accept any delivery, and does not control how a courier performs one.

A Courier may, entirely at their option, signal that they are **available**. Signaling availability:
- is a lightweight, instantly-reversible toggle;
- imposes **no** obligation to remain reachable, to stay in any location, or to accept any opportunity;
- carries **no** penalty for not confirming an opportunity that appears while available;
- leaves the Courier free to do anything else, including work for others, in the meantime.

The Platform's role is limited to facilitating communication and payment among Senders, couriers, and customers.

> **▸ Rationale (capacity-first).** Orders are matched to *already-willing* couriers rather than dumped on an orphaned queue that someone must be pressured to clear. This removes any need for acceptance quotas or decline penalties. The availability toggle is deliberately drafted as "waiting to be engaged," **not** "engaged to wait" — couriers are never held on-call at the Platform's direction, which would both imply employment and create wage exposure.

## 2. Courier Eligibility — the three age gates
Couriers must complete identity verification, provide valid government identification, and complete onboarding. Minimum age depends on the activity:

| Tier / activity | Minimum age |
|---|---|
| **Deliver** (foot, bicycle, scooter — no motor vehicle) | 16 |
| **Drive** (any motor vehicle) | 18 |
| **Alcohol** (any delivery containing alcohol) | **21** |

The alcohol gate (§8) applies **on top of** the tier gate: an alcohol-bearing delivery may be performed only by a Courier who is at least 21, regardless of tier. Additional verification may be required for expanded services.

> **▸ Rationale (alcohol 21+).** Missouri law (RSMo 311.300.2) prohibits anyone under 21 from delivering intoxicating liquor away from the licensed premises. The prior single "16+" eligibility silently conflicted with alcohol delivery; the gates are now explicit. `‹rider›` — the alcohol-delivery age is statutory per state.

## 3. Transportation
Couriers may use bicycle, walking, scooter, car, or other lawful transportation, and are responsible for maintaining it.

### 3.1 Legal Compliance
Couriers comply with all applicable laws and traffic regulations while performing deliveries.

## 4. Insurance
Couriers operating motor vehicles must maintain all legally-required insurance **and** coverage valid for commercial/delivery use (a personal auto policy that excludes delivery use is not sufficient). Couriers assume all risks associated with their transportation.

> **▸ Rationale (insurance).** Personal auto policies almost universally exclude delivery/commercial use, so the Courier's indemnity (§13) is only as collectible as the Courier. The Platform's real protection is its **own** commercial general-liability + contingent / non-owned-auto coverage, carried separately from this Agreement. Motor-vehicle couriers should carry a delivery endorsement.

## 5. Delivery Claiming
Senders submit delivery requests through the Platform; those opportunities become visible to available couriers, who may independently choose to claim them. When a Courier claims an opportunity, the Courier agrees to make a good-faith effort to complete it or — if it cannot be completed — to return the order to the Sender or follow reasonable resolution instructions provided through the Platform.

### 5.1 Pickup Confirmation
After claiming, a Courier who intends to complete the delivery is expected to confirm pickup promptly. If pickup is not confirmed within a reasonable time, the Platform may return the opportunity to the queue for another courier.

### 5.2 Order Handling
Couriers transport orders with reasonable care, deliver them in the condition received from the Sender, and must not open, tamper with, or alter any order.

## 6. Declining and Cancellation — free to decline, accountable for what you claim
A Courier is **free to decline any opportunity, at any time, for any reason, with no penalty.** The Platform does not impose acceptance-rate requirements and does not penalize declining.

A Courier *is* accountable for a delivery they **affirmatively claim**: having claimed it, they undertake to complete it in good faith or to return/resolve it under §5. Repeatedly claiming deliveries and then abandoning them — leaving customers and Senders stranded — may result in warnings, temporary restrictions, or removal, addressed relationally and with notice under §14.

> **▸ Rationale (the §6 recut — the crux).** Penalizing a contractor for *declining offered work* is among the strongest factors pushing toward employee status across every classification test, and it erodes trust. The accountable event is therefore **never** "you declined too many," but only "you claimed this one and abandoned it" — a specific obligation the Courier *chose* to undertake, with a neighbor's order now depending on it. Freedom at the door; commitment past the threshold. In a bounded neighborhood with a minimum order, trips are roughly equal in value, so there is little to cherry-pick and little to police — the operating conditions, not a penalty regime, carry reliability.

## 7. Courier Compensation
Courier compensation is derived from the service charge on completed deliveries: **75% `‹rider: fee schedule›` of the service charge** is paid to the Courier performing the delivery. **Customer tips belong entirely to the Courier.** Payments are typically distributed through automated nightly payouts via the Platform's third-party payment system.

The Courier is responsible for their own taxes and will receive an IRS Form 1099 where required; the Platform does not withhold taxes.

> **▸ Rationale (1099 / own-taxes).** Stating tax responsibility and 1099 issuance reinforces IC status and sets expectations. Tip protection (100% to courier) is increasingly legally required and is stated explicitly.

## 8. Alcohol Deliveries
A delivery containing alcohol may be performed only by a Courier who is **at least 21** (§2). For such deliveries the Courier acts as the Sender's agent at the door and must:
- verify a valid, unexpired government ID by comparing its photo and physical characteristics to the recipient (RSMo 311.328 `‹rider›`);
- refuse delivery if the recipient is underage or intoxicated; and
- on refusal, return the alcohol to the Sender.

The Sender (not the Courier) is the licensed seller and is responsible for filling and sealing alcohol per law (§9 of the Sender Agreement). The Courier does not fill, package, or seal alcohol. Failure to comply may result in removal.

> **▸ Rationale (alcohol agency).** Under RSMo 311.202 the licensed retailer's 21+ staff fills and tamper-seals the container; the Platform is not a liquor licensee. The Courier's only alcohol role is transport + the door-side ID check, performed as the Sender's agent. `‹rider›` — ID-verification and age rules are statutory per state.

## 9. Conduct
Couriers act respectfully toward customers, Senders, and other couriers. The Platform may remove couriers for conduct that harms the service.

## 10. Theft or Misconduct
Intentional interference with deliveries may result in immediate removal. The Platform may investigate incidents before determining outcomes.

## 11. Privacy
The Platform minimizes collection of customer data. Couriers agree not to store, retain, disclose, or misuse customer information except as necessary to complete a delivery.

## 12. Assumption of Risk and Limitation of Liability
Couriers perform deliveries at their own risk and are responsible for their transportation choices and legal compliance. The Platform is not responsible for injuries, accidents, property damage, or other incidents arising from courier transportation, nor for food preparation, food safety, or order accuracy.

## 13. Indemnification
The Courier agrees to defend, indemnify, and hold harmless the Platform and its owners, officers, employees, and agents from claims, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, or expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising out of or related to: the Courier's transportation activities; accidents or injuries during delivery; violations of traffic or other laws; failure to verify identification or comply with alcohol requirements; theft, tampering, or mishandling of orders; or the Courier's breach of this Agreement. This applies to claims brought by customers, Senders, regulators, or other third parties. The indemnifying party agrees to cooperate reasonably in the defense of any such claim.

## 14. Platform Authority
The Platform may suspend, restrict, or remove access to protect the safety, reliability, and integrity of the service. Removal decisions are handled relationally and with notice and an opportunity to respond, except where immediate action is required for safety or law.

> **▸ Rationale (relational deactivation).** Algorithmic, ratings-driven deactivation reads as employer control. Notice-and-respond handling both fits a neighborhood-scale, trust-based relationship and is gentler on the classification analysis.

## 15. Service Availability
The Platform is not liable for delays or failures caused by circumstances beyond its control, including weather, technical outages, Sender closures, or courier availability.

## 16. Dispute Resolution
The parties first attempt to resolve disputes informally. If that fails, either party may bring a claim in a court of competent jurisdiction in the State of Missouri `‹rider: governing jurisdiction›`, including small-claims court where applicable.

> **▸ Rationale (no arbitration / no class waiver).** Deliberately omitted, by design — declining the gig-platform's mandatory-arbitration + class-waiver playbook. At neighborhood scale the safeguards are substantive (being a genuine marketplace, a friendly forum, small scale, non-aggrieved couriers), not procedural. Revisit only if the operation scales into a litigation target. `‹rider›` — for an instance in a jurisdiction whose worker-protection law cannot be contracted around, the *courier relationship* is governed by the law of the place the work is performed (see Rider §1).
