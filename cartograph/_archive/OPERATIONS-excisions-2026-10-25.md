- ⭐ **The shore median** *(ruled 2026-10-04)* — the ground between the **drawn shoreline** and the **lidar's waterline**, read at the elevation source's finest pixel. The waterline is **traced as one continuous line** (marching squares on a 1 m grid), and the median is where the drawing and the lidar disagree, touching the shore. The bake prints its area each way, its mean width, where the two lines drift farthest apart, and the hectares where the lidar has **no value** (not known, not drawn). **See it:** Stage (or Preview) › Diagnostic › *Shore median* — session-only, never saved — paints it solid with the revetment hidden — orange where the lidar ground runs into the drawn water, magenta where the lidar water reaches behind the shore, its edges in cyan. **In the slab it is the town's sand:** the ground bake paints the median as a `shore` sand layer in the one ground plane, above the land-use and below every road, only when the median was walked on the shoreline that bake writes. A town that draws a coast and has no such median **refuses to bake**, naming the file. The one exception is the Bake's first ground pass (`--shore=pending`), which writes the shoreline the median is walked on and stamps the slab `shore: "pending"`; the Bake then walks the median and re-runs the ground (`ground-shore`) without the flag, only when that first pass stamped `pending` or the median was re-walked in this Bake; an inland town or a current median skips it, by name. ▶ `node cartograph/bake-shore-median.mjs --scene=<id> [--out=<dir>]`
