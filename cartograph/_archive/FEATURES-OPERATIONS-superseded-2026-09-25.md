## FEATURES — the two paragraphs before the 2026-09-25 trim

**The ground has the shape the ground actually has.** The kit finds the town's own laser survey — the same national lidar a surveyor would order — and pours the real relief: the rise of a street, the bank behind a seawall, the roll of a dune. Nobody types a height and nobody downloads a gigabyte; the kit reads only the few megabytes of the survey it needs. **Where the survey does not reach, the kit says so** rather than quietly flattening the town: a place with no lidar is marked as searched-and-absent, and a flat pour becomes a decision someone made instead of a silence nobody noticed. ⭐ And it takes **one** survey rather than the best pieces of several, because butting a 2015 flight against a 2021 one seams the ground exactly where it genuinely moved — which on a barrier beach is most of what you came to see. ⭐ **And the hills are lit by their own slope**: the sun shades a bank the way it shades a real one, because the lighting reads the same relief the ground is drawn with, at that survey's own spacing — until 2026-09-24 every town's ground was raised into its hills and then lit as if it were a table. *(As-built: `fetch-dem.mjs`; the heightfield is `bake-terrain.js`; the lighting is `terrainShader.js` `TERRAIN_NORMAL`, guarded by `checks/claims-ground-normals-come-from-the-terrain.mjs`.)*

**Everything sits exactly on the ground — one surface, nothing floats.** Trees, lamps, gravel paths — every object meets the terrain precisely, because they all anchor to the *single rendered ground surface* (a baked per-object anchor) rather than each guessing its own height off a smooth field the coarse ground only approximates. On a rolling site like Lafayette Park that's the difference between a believable place and a diorama of objects hovering over a hill — a trunk half a meter off the grass reads instantly as fake. The same discipline already seats the buildings (corner-mean foundations); it now extends to everything that stands on the ground. ⭐ Each anchor file also records **which ground it was measured on**, so a re-surveyed terrain can never leave trees quietly floating on the old one — the map refuses the stale anchors by name and says how to re-bake them. *(As-built: `ARCHITECTURE.md §8` "Ground conformance"; guarded by `checks/claims-anchors-know-their-terrain.mjs`.)*
