What goes into a town

This place was not generated. It was assembled — from public record, from measurements taken on the street, and from a quantity of hand-work there is no way around. Here is the whole list, and where each piece comes from.

The ground you stand on

Streets first. Every curb, every corner and every block face is a consequence of where the street centreline runs, so that line is the one thing worth getting right.

Street centrelines, buildings, land use
OpenStreetMap, queried once and kept on disk. Nothing here phones home while you look at it.
Public recordODbL
Corrected centrelines
Where the public map had a street in the wrong place, it was redrawn by hand against aerial imagery.
Made by hand35 streets
Street widths
Roadway and right-of-way measured on site, because the published defaults are a national average and this is not a national average.
Made by hand
Building footprints
Machine-learning outlines derived from aerial imagery in the US; in Europe, the hand-mapped public record is the better source and is used instead.
Public record
Building detail — storeys, roofs, materials
What turns a field of boxes into a street. Where the public record carries it, it is read straight off. Where it does not, it is entered building by building.

The trees

Every tree stands where a real tree stands. The species come from the city's own inventory; what each species looks like is a separate and much longer problem.

The tree census
Municipal forestry inventories — species, trunk diameter, condition, per tree. Four separate surveys, covering different ground, stitched into one.
Public record4 surveys
Canopy cover
Satellite-derived tree cover, used to fill yards and parkland no per-tree survey reaches. Some towns decline it and ship only counted trees.
Public record
Bark and leaf reference
Photographic reference, all of it public-domain. The leaves that had no public equivalent were collected in a bag and put through a flatbed scanner.
Public recordCC0 · 27 sets
Species dossiers
A written profile per species — habit, branching, seasonal colour, how it actually ages — scored on a fixed rubric so two species can be compared rather than merely described.
Made by hand19 axes
Tree structure
A tree's identity is mostly its branching, and branching is the one thing you cannot compose from parts. Each is chosen from a library and tagged by eye, one at a time.
Made by hand~241 forms

The sky

Most of this needed no acquisition at all. Given a latitude, a longitude and a date, the sky is arithmetic — which is the whole reason it works anywhere.

Sun, moon, twilight, seasons
Sunrise, solar noon, moon phase and the tilt of the celestial pole, all derived. Below the equator the stars wheel the other way, correctly, with nothing fetched.
Computed
Stars, constellations, planets
A published bright-star catalogue, the constellation figures, and the orbital elements for the planets. Acquired once; true for every town thereafter.
Public record523 stars
Weather
Live conditions drive cloud, rain, wetness and how hard the trees are moving. A published reanalysis archive reaching back to 1940 supplies the local climate underneath it.
Public recordCC BY 4.0
Cloud forms and weather rules
Cloud shapes authored once, then matched to conditions by rules written on physics rather than on any one place — cumulus is cumulus everywhere.
Made by hand52 forms

The town's own record

The part no dataset holds. It is joined on afterwards and keyed to a stable public identifier, so the map underneath can be rebuilt from scratch without disturbing it.

Historic designation
In the United States, a National Register nomination — read out of the scanned document page by page. In Europe the national heritage register is already on the public map, which is a considerably shorter afternoon.
Public record
Businesses, hours, menus
A public listings base, then corrected and filled in by hand — the hours a place actually keeps, what it serves, what it is for.
Made by hand
Photographs and marks
Sourced with permission and self-hosted, never hotlinked. Where a business genuinely has no logo, that is recorded as a finding so nobody spends the afternoon again.
Made by hand
What the neighbours know
Which building matters, and why. There is no file for this anywhere, and it is the part the people who live here supply.
Made by hand

Every one of these is a file kept on disk. Fetching is how a thing is acquired, once — never how the place is drawn. Unplug the network and the town still pours, which is the point.

Absent means absent. A town with no lamp survey has no lamps; a town with no tree census has no trees. Nothing quietly stands in for something a place does not have, and nothing is borrowed from a neighbourhood on the other side of the world.

Counts shown are Lafayette Square, St. Louis — the most complete of these to date.
